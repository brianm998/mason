# Stage 23: Notifications, sound and speech

Summary: Alerts now reach the owner without becoming noise: a routing table with Part 19's defaults sends one notice per channel and one reminder, then stays silent once the condition clears or is acknowledged; monitors queue a sound then one sentence, never overlapping, with one device speaking and mute leaving every visual alert; a lost daemon connection is raised by the monitor itself; and email (SMTP), ntfy and a signed webhook each pass a conformance kit.

## Built
- `crates/core/src/notify.rs` (pure): the routing table as data with Part 19's defaults, per-condition overrides, quiet-hours windows, `due` (what is owed now: first notices after their delay, one reminder, bounded retries, nothing once acknowledged), the sentence builder, the sound family per condition, speaker election (`elect`, 30 s takeover) and the daemon-speech rule. Unit tests for each default and rule.
- Migration 18 and `crates/store/src/notify.rs`: `alerts.acked_at` and `reminded_at`, `deliveries` (every try), `announcements` (the queue, unique key so each happens once).
- `crates/adapter-api/src/notify_kit.rs` and `notify_channels.rs`: the `Notifier` trait with classified errors (`Transient`, `Permanent`), `conformance`, and the channels: `Email` (over a `MailTransport`; `CurlMail` runs `curl smtp://` with settings and password on stdin), `Ntfy`, `Webhook` (`X-Mason-Signature`, verifies with the existing `webhook::verify`).
- `crates/daemon/src/notify.rs`: `notify.toml` (routing, quiet, reminder, speech, email, ntfy, webhook), the dispatcher run on every watcher pass after `sync_alerts`, the announcement queue and speaker election behind `POST /announce/claim`, landed announcements, daemon speech through a configured command, test sends. HTTP: `POST /alerts/{key}/ack` and `/alerts/ack` (owner only), `POST /announce/claim`, `GET /notify`, `POST /notify/test`; `GET /alerts` shows `acked_at` and `reminded_at`. CLI: `mason alerts ack KEY`, `mason notify [status]`, `mason notify test CHANNEL`.
- SwiftUI and Compose: `Speech.swift` / `Speech.kt` (an `Announcer` queue behind a `SpeechSink`, sound per family, mute gating, claim handling, the 15 s "daemon down" clock, settings stored per device), system sinks in each app (`AVSpeechSynthesizer` and `NSSound`; `say`/`afplay`, `espeak`, PowerShell on the desktop), a settings popover/dialog (conditions, sound per family, voices from the OS), a "Daemon down" banner, a 1 s pulse that claims every 3 s. Four rows in `clients/features.toml`.
- `scripts/exit/s23.sh`, `s23-real.sh`, `s23-manual.sh`, fixtures in `scripts/exit/fixtures/s23/fakes.py`, decision 0050.

## Exit test
`bash scripts/exit/s23.sh` ends with `s23 OK` (about 2 minutes, most of it the 30 s takeover wait, twice). With a real `masond`, fake SMTP, ntfy and webhook receivers and two fake monitors (plus a muted one) on loopback, and a fake speech command:
1. `mason notify` prints Part 19's default table. A pause (routed to every channel for this script) produces one alert, one email, one ntfy push, one webhook and one announcement; the webhook signature verifies and a wrong secret does not; no secret appears in a message or the daemon log. After the 4 s reminder delay there is exactly one more of each (the second webhook says `reminder: true`); six seconds later nothing more. Clearing it: nothing more.
2. A second alert acknowledged straight away produces no reminder or webhook, is still listed ("acknowledged"), and a non-owner's acknowledgement is refused (403). Clearing and returning is a new alert with a new notice.
3. Two alerts at once queue two announcements. Of two monitors connected over the WebSocket, exactly one is told to speak and gets both, in order, as sound, sentence, sound, sentence with no overlap in the fake sink's timestamps; a muted third monitor never speaks; the alerts are still listed while it is muted.
4. The speaker is killed and the alerts cleared; after 31 s a new alert is spoken by the other monitor.
5. With `speech.daemon = true` and a monitor claiming, the daemon says nothing. With every monitor gone for 33 s it speaks two queued sentences in order, one finishing before the next starts.
In addition: `cargo test` has the pure-rule unit tests (core), store tests, the conformance runs for each channel, `crates/daemon/tests/notify.rs` (seven tests: one alert/one delivery/one reminder/silence with a real webhook receiver and curl; acknowledge and return; election; mute; daemon speech on and off), `swift test` (SpeechTests, 11 tests, 67 in all) and `gradle :core:test` (SpeechTest, 11 tests) using a fake sink that finishes only when told, so overlap would fail the test.

## What testing found
- **Concurrent `sync_alerts` calls could clear and re-raise a live alert.** The watcher and `GET /alerts` each work out what is true and then write it; the older answer can clear an alert the newer one just raised, and the next pass raises it again with a new start, so the owner would have been notified twice. The first `s23.sh` run showed two announcements for one pause at different times. `sync_alerts` now holds a lock across both steps. This changes existing code in `attention.rs`.
- **A monitor opened later was handed old announcements** for alerts that had since cleared. Announcements about a cleared or acknowledged alert are now dropped before they are handed out.
- My first script waited on `test "$(count ...)" -ge N`, which evaluates the count once; the waits now call functions.
- `clippy` and `fmt` were clean after the above.

## Not done, and not verified
- **Nothing was heard.** No speaker was used: the speech sinks (`AVSpeechSynthesizer`, `NSSound`, `say`, `afplay`, `espeak`, PowerShell) are built and compile (`swift build`, `gradle :app:compileKotlin`) but were never run against a real device. Voice listing from the OS, the real sounds and the settings popover/dialog were not looked at. `s23-manual.sh` lists what to check; it was not run.
- **No real service.** The real `curl smtp://` path was exercised against a loopback fake SMTP server only (no TLS, no authentication offered by the fake, so the password was not transmitted; the STARTTLS path and a real server's login are untested). ntfy and the webhook were exercised against loopback fakes. `s23-real.sh` (your SMTP server, ntfy topic, webhook and speech command) needs `MASON_REAL_APPROVED=yes` and refuses without it; it was not run.
- **The condition used in `s23.sh` is a pause with its routing overridden.** A pause is cheap to raise and clear, and it proves the mechanism; the defaults for needs-attention, failed and daemon-down are checked by unit tests and by the printed table, not by raising those conditions end to end. No fake-agent task was driven to "needs a decision" or "failed" for this stage.
- **Provider outage and credential failures have routes but no alert source** (decision 0050).
- **The fake monitors speak the WebSocket protocol but are Python, not the real clients.** The real clients' claim loop (`pulse`) is unit-tested with a fake sink, not run against a daemon. Two real monitors were never open at once.
- **The daemon's own "daemon down" speech when the daemon itself has crashed** is not possible by construction; ntfy or email for that case needs a watcher outside the daemon, which this stage does not build.
- Quiet hours were exercised by unit tests and the settings parser, not in an end-to-end run, and local time comes from `date`.
- Sends run on the watcher thread (decision 0050).
- **Protected files.** `clients/features.toml` is a parity gate (docs/self-hosting.md); four rows were added, all `done` in both clients, which waits for the owner's approval. No test, limit, budget or verdict logic was changed or loosened, and nothing was ignored or removed.
