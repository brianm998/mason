# s260: waiting jobs share one scheduling pass, and the home view takes the broker lock once

Summary: A job waiting in `Broker::acquire` now skips its own scheduling pass when one ran less than 100 ms ago, and the home view reads every task's model-session lease under one hold of the broker lock instead of one per task. Landed by hand while the pipeline was blocked on exactly this; #284 carries the fuller change (probes outside the lock).

## Why

On 2026-11-13, after the 15:46 self-update, 41 jobs were waiting for resources. Each woke every 100 ms (and on every grant's `notify_all`) and ran the whole `pass` under the broker mutex: about 400 serialized passes a second on a machine at load 60. A 5 s `sample` of masond showed the views thread waiting on that mutex 70% of the time in `Broker::llm_held_by`, which `Ctx::home` called once per task. `/health` took 19 s and `/views/home` over 20 s, so monitor and `mason run` WebSockets were reset after about 20 s. It looked as if the daemon kept restarting, and no interactive build could get a slot.

## What changed

- `crates/daemon/src/sched.rs`: `State.last_pass` and `State.passes`, set at the end of `pass`. In `acquire`'s wait loop the pass runs only when `pass_due` (none in the last `PASS_EVERY`, 100 ms). The `holds(id)` check and `wait_timeout` still share one hold of the lock, and everything stays under the lock, so no grant can be made twice. Releases, submits and lease polls still pass at once, and every grant still wakes all waiters.
- `Broker::llm_holders()`: the set of tasks holding an `llm:` lease, under one hold. The home view in `views.rs` uses it.
- Tests: `a_waiters_pass_is_due_only_when_none_ran_lately`, and `waiting_jobs_share_one_pass_and_all_get_their_turn` (30 waiters; passes are counted against the waiters' own liveness checks, not the clock; then every waiter is granted in turn).

## Not verified

The effect on the live 41-job daemon is only seen after the self-update takes over. Time-based gates (CPU, settle pause, dead outside holders) are still re-evaluated at most every 100 ms, now by whichever waiter passes rather than by all of them. The full workspace test run was not repeated here; the broker unit tests and the scheduling integration tests were.
