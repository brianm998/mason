# s85: hold new work and alert when the disk is nearly full

Summary: Below a free-disk floor (`[disk] floor_gb`, default 20) nothing new compiles or opens an LLM session, `mason resources` says why, an alert names the free space and what takes room, and `task_cache` ages out cancelled and failed tasks' directories after 7 days.

What changed:

- `resources.toml` `[disk] floor_gb` (default 20; 0 turns it off). Free space is the least over the data directory's volume and each project's `task_cache` volume, read at most every 30 s through an injectable source (`MASON_DISK_FILE` holds the free GB, `DaemonConfig::disk` for tests; `MASON_DISK_EVERY_MS` shortens the interval for the exit script).
- Scheduler: `DiskGate` in the plan and `WaitCause::Disk`, "Waiting: disk has 12 GB free, floor 20 GB". It holds a request that needs `compile` or any LLM resource, with or without other work running. Running work and landing are not touched.
- Alert `disk` (in `alerts_now`) while free space is below the floor or a store write was refused for want of disk (cleared by the next successful write). It names the free space, the floor, and the `task_cache` and worktree sizes, measured on a background thread and reused for 5 minutes.
- `taskcache::sweep` also removes a cancelled or failed task's directory once its last step is older than `task_cache_keep_days` (project file, default 7). A task that has a job, or has been revived, is not touched.

Tests: scheduler (below the floor nothing is granted and the wait says why; at the floor work is granted; other resources and running work are unaffected), `tests/disk.rs` (a real daemon with the scripted agent), `tests/taskcache.rs` (older goes, younger stays, revived untouched), unit tests for the readers. Exit script: `scripts/exit/s85.sh`.

Not verified: the real `statvfs` reading is only checked to return a number on this machine, not against a genuinely full volume; no store write was made to fail with a real ENOSPC (the recognition is tested on a constructed SQLite `SQLITE_FULL` error); the sizes in the alert were not measured against the owner's 126 GB tree; the `masond` binary with `MASON_DISK_FILE` was not run by hand.
