# s1139: a die-temperature or power-limit hold says what it is

Summary: A thermal wait now names the condition that holds work and what releases it (die temperature against `thermal_max_c`, speed against `thermal_min_pct`, or SMC power limiting) in the waiting line, the stored wait event, the queue and home-page lines and `mason resources`, instead of "work starts at 101% or more".

## Cause

The scheduler's `ThermalGate` was a speed against a minimum, so `sched.rs` carried a die or power-limit hold as `min_pct = speed + 1` (101 at full speed), and every sentence made from the gate printed it. Only the home-page compile reason used the real cause (s700 left this open).

## Change

- `crates/scheduler`: `ThermalGate` has a `hold` field (`ThermalHold`: `Speed`, `Die`, `PowerLimited`, `Ceiling`); `min_pct` is the configured minimum again. `thermal_sentence` and `thermal_short` are the only places a thermal hold is put into words. `Ledger::set_heat_ceiling` takes the die reading and the ceiling, so a held update build names the ceiling.
- `crates/daemon`: `cpu_gate` fills `hold` from `thermal::hot`; the home-page compile reason and the queue-held line use `thermal_short`. The snapshot's `thermal_min_pct` is the configured value during a die hold.
- Wait events stored before this change keep their old wording.

## Not verified

- No run against the real SMC; the tests inject the readings.
