# s457: iOS pairing screens: scan the QR code, or pick a nearby daemon and type its code

Summary: The iOS app shows a pairing screen when nothing is paired (QR scan with VisionKit, or a Bonjour list plus the 8-digit code), opens `mason://pair` links, and has a Settings sheet with Forget.

- `MonitorCore/PairingScreen.swift`: `PairingScreenState` (pure: scan result, typed code via `Pin.isCode`, choose, begin/fail/succeed), `PairingMessage` (refusal status plus the daemon's own message in words: 400 wrong, 410 expired, 429 locked, 401/403 refused), and `PairingModel` (runs `Pairing.pair`, saves the `PairedRecord`, calls `RemoteSession.pairingChanged`; `forget` calls `unpair`).
- `MonitorPhone/PairingViews.swift`: `PairingView`, `NearbyList`, `PairedDaemonSettingsView`, and the VisionKit `QRScannerView` (QR only, iOS only).
- `ios/MasonIOS`: `NSCameraUsageDescription`, the `mason` URL scheme, and the app wiring (`RemoteSession`, Bonjour source, scene-phase activate/deactivate, `onOpenURL`, a gear that opens Settings).
- The Nearby list says scanning is the stronger check and shows the first 8 characters of the fingerprint to compare with `mason lan status`.
- Tests: `PairingScreenTests` in `MonitorCoreTests`.

**Not verified.** The sandbox this was written in had no network, so SwiftPM could not fetch swift-protobuf: neither `swift test` nor `make ios` was run here, and the new Swift was not compiled. The camera view, the URL scheme hand-off, the Settings sheet and the layout on a real device were not seen; no offscreen render of the pairing screen was made. Pairing a real phone stays the owner's check (`scripts/exit/s24-real.sh`).
